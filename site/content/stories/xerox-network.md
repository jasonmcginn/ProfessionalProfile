<section>
<p class="sec-label">// 01 the situation</p>
<h2>The fault isn't always the printer</h2>
<p class="lead">On a Xerox call, the fault isn't always the printer.</p>
<p>When it turns out to be the network, technically out of my scope, I don't like leaving a customer stuck.</p>
</section>
<section>
<p class="sec-label">// 02 what I did</p>
<h2>Trace it, flag it, fix it with their OK</h2>
<ol class="parts">
<li>
<div class="part-h">
<span class="n">1</span><h3>Trace it</h3>
</div>
<p>A bank couldn't send print jobs to one of their machines. I traced it back through the patch panel to the port and saw it right there: the panel's ethernet cable was unplugged.</p>
</li>
<li>
<div class="part-h">
<span class="n">2</span><h3>Flag it, and fix it with their OK</h3>
</div>
<p>I flagged it to their IT, and with their OK I plugged it back in and got a link light. A reboot of the printer and it was back on the network.</p>
</li>
<li>
<div class="part-h">
<span class="n">3</span><h3>Find the cause</h3>
</div>
<p>The cause turned out to be their ISP, who had been in doing work and left that cable out by mistake.</p>
</li>
</ol>
</section>
<section>
<p class="sec-label">// 03 what I take from it</p>
<h2>Diagnose, document, hand it over</h2>
<p class="lead">It isn't my system to change, so I diagnose, document, and hand the fix to their IT to approve, often working it through with them directly.</p>
<p>It respects the boundary, and it's the kind of work that bridges this field role to the network and systems role I'm aiming for.</p>
</section>
