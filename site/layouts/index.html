// 01 how I work

One approach, applied to very different problems.

Twenty-plus years across networking, systems, cloud and automation. The problems change; the core way I work them doesn't.
These five principles are that approach, in the order I apply them, each one shown in real work.

  1. Know it to the core.I take neglected systems apart until I know them cold, then rebuild them so a fault traces cleanly from the wall jack to the switch port. Untangling the mess is how I come to trust it.see it in the rack rebuilds →
  2. Secure it by default.I find exactly what a system needs to run, then grant only that and lock the rest down. Least privilege, enforced by policy rather than trust.see it in the Sage lab →
  3. Deliver it methodically.Diagnose, document, propose, verify, in that order. I work a problem end to end and write it up, so the fix is something anyone can check and repeat.see it in the kindergarten proposal →
  4. Build it around people.I don't just run technology; I shape it so it works for people without them having to think about it. Nothing beats watching someone get time back every time a solution I rolled out kicks in.see it in the QoS fix →
  5. Build the tools that build the next ones.Tools exist to move you toward a goal, and getting there usually takes tools working with other tools. I like building the one that makes the next one easier.see it in the account provisioning →

// 06 beyond the keyboard

A maker, on and off the clock.

I'm a maker at heart. I like building things that have a real use and purpose, with a design that is both elegant and functional. When I'm away from the keyboard I'm usually in the woods, or out in the shop making something with my hands.

A lot of that happens at the CNC, where I enjoy the design side as much as the carving. I designed my own cribbage board and have sold dozens of them. I also turn and carve bowls, pens, and plenty of other woodworking projects, tons of them over the years. And I love dogs.

It's the same instinct that shapes how I build systems. I want what I make to last, and to actually get used.
